# Retrospectives

## When to Use

- Running a sprint retrospective
- Facilitating a quarterly or project-level retrospective
- Choosing a retro format for a specific team situation
- Following up on retro action items
- Recognizing patterns across multiple retros
- Improving a team's retro culture when retros feel stale or unproductive

---

## Core Principles

1. **Psychological safety first** — People must feel safe to be honest. No blame, no retaliation.
2. **Actionable outcomes** — Every retro produces 1–3 concrete action items with owners and deadlines.
3. **Variety keeps engagement** — Rotate formats to prevent retro fatigue.
4. **Follow through** — Start every retro by reviewing last retro's action items. Nothing kills retro culture faster than ignored actions.
5. **Timebox** — 60 minutes for a sprint retro, 90 minutes for a quarterly retro. Respect the clock.

## Sprint Retrospective (60 minutes)

### Standard Flow

| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 0:00–0:05 | **Check-in**: Review previous action items — completed? In progress? Dropped? |
| 0:05–0:10 | **Set the stage**: State the retro format, remind of ground rules |
| 0:10–0:30 | **Gather data**: Team writes and shares observations (format-dependent) |
| 0:30–0:45 | **Generate insights**: Group, discuss, and prioritize themes |
| 0:45–0:55 | **Decide actions**: Pick 1–3 action items with owners and due dates |
| 0:55–1:00 | **Close**: Quick round — one word for how you feel about the sprint |

### Ground Rules (share at start)

- What's said in retro stays in retro
- Focus on process and systems, not individuals
- Everyone participates — no spectators
- Disagree respectfully; assume good intent
- The goal is improvement, not venting

## Retro Formats

Rotate formats every 2–4 sprints to keep things fresh.

### 1. Start / Stop / Continue

**Best for**: General-purpose, new teams, quick retros

| Column | Prompt |
|--------|--------|
| **Start** | What should we start doing that we're not doing? |
| **Stop** | What should we stop doing because it's not helping? |
| **Continue** | What's working well that we should keep doing? |

### 2. 4Ls (Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed For)

**Best for**: After a major release or project milestone

| Column | Prompt |
|--------|--------|
| **Liked** | What did you enjoy or appreciate? |
| **Learned** | What did you learn this sprint? |
| **Lacked** | What was missing or insufficient? |
| **Longed For** | What do you wish you had? |

### 3. Mad / Sad / Glad

**Best for**: Emotional check-in after a tough sprint, or when morale seems low

| Column | Prompt |
|--------|--------|
| **Mad** | What frustrated you? |
| **Sad** | What disappointed you? |
| **Glad** | What made you happy? |

### 4. Sailboat

**Best for**: Visual thinkers, identifying forces helping and hindering the team

Draw a sailboat:
- **Wind** (sails): What propelled us forward?
- **Anchor**: What held us back?
- **Rocks** (underwater): What risks do we see ahead?
- **Island** (destination): Where are we trying to go?

### 5. Timeline

**Best for**: Quarterly retros or after a long project

Create a timeline of the sprint/quarter. Team adds sticky notes for:
- Highs (green) — moments of success or energy
- Lows (red) — moments of frustration or failure
- Key events (blue) — incidents, launches, decisions

Discuss patterns in the timeline.

### 6. One-Word Check-In + Fishbowl

**Best for**: Large teams (8+), deep-dive on a specific topic

1. Everyone shares one word describing the sprint
2. Facilitator picks the dominant theme
3. 3–4 volunteers discuss in a "fishbowl" (inner circle) while others observe
4. Outer circle can rotate in

## Quarterly Retrospective (90 minutes)

### Purpose

A quarterly retro looks at patterns across sprints, not individual sprint issues. It's about systemic improvements.

### Agenda

| Time | Activity |
|------|----------|
| 0:00–0:10 | Review quarterly OKR scores and delivery metrics |
| 0:10–0:20 | Review action items from previous quarterly retro |
| 0:20–0:50 | Timeline exercise across the quarter — highs, lows, key events |
| 0:50–1:10 | Theme discussion — What patterns do we see? What systemic issues? |
| 1:10–1:25 | Action items — 2–4 systemic improvements with owners |
| 1:25–1:30 | Close — Appreciation round (each person appreciates one teammate) |

## Action Item Tracking

### Format

```markdown
## Retro Action Items — Sprint [N] / Q[N]

| # | Action | Owner | Due Date | Status |
|---|--------|-------|----------|--------|
| 1 | [Action] | @name | YYYY-MM-DD | Open |
| 2 | [Action] | @name | YYYY-MM-DD | Open |
| 3 | [Action] | @name | YYYY-MM-DD | Open |
```

### Rules

- **Max 3 action items per retro** — More than 3 won't get done
- **Every action has an owner** — Shared ownership means no ownership
- **Every action has a due date** — "Sometime" means never
- **Review at next retro** — First agenda item, every time
- **Track completion rate** — If <50% of actions complete, you have a follow-through problem

## Facilitation Tips

1. **Rotate the facilitator** — Don't always facilitate yourself as EM. Give team members the opportunity.
2. **Use a timer** — Visible countdown keeps discussions on track.
3. **Silent brainstorming first** — Give 5 minutes of silent writing before discussion. This prevents anchoring and gives introverts space.
4. **Dot vote for prioritization** — Each person gets 3 votes to place on themes. Discuss the top 2–3.
5. **Remote-friendly tools** — Use a collaborative whiteboard tool (Miro, FigJam, or equivalent) for distributed teams.
6. **As the manager, speak last** — Your opinions carry weight; let the team share first.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Problem | Better Approach |
|-------------|---------|----------------|
| Same format every time | Team tunes out, engagement drops | Rotate formats every 2–4 sprints |
| No action items | Retro becomes a venting session | Always end with 1–3 actions with owners |
| Actions never reviewed | Trust in the retro process erodes | Start every retro reviewing last retro's actions |
| Manager dominates | Team stops sharing honestly | Manager speaks last, facilitator rotates |
| Blame individuals | Kills psychological safety | Focus on process and systems, not people |
| Skipping retros | Problems compound, morale drops | Retros are non-negotiable; reschedule, don't cancel |
| Too many action items | Nothing gets done | Cap at 3; do fewer things well |
